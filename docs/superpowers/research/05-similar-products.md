# Similar Products — Table Stakes vs Differentiating

> Research stream: competitive landscape for CatatMD. Scope: what ambient/AI clinical documentation vendors claim about PHI handling and residency, what they charge, what they **refuse** to do, and what they output — then which of CatatMD's five claims survive contact with the market.
>
> Method: vendor security/trust/legal pages and regulator documentation fetched directly wherever possible. Every claim carries a URL. Evidence is graded:
>
> - **VERIFIED** — read on the vendor's or regulator's own page, quoted.
> - **INFERRED** — from a search snippet, a secondary outlet, or reasoning across two verified facts. Flagged inline.
> - **NOT FOUND** — looked for it, did not find it. Stated as such rather than guessed.

---

## 1. Executive Verdict

| CatatMD Claim                              | Verdict                                                 | One-Line Reason                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Doctor approves everything                 | **Table stakes**                                        | Universal. Microsoft, Tortus, Heidi and even the local Malaysian entrant all state it as a hard requirement.                                    |
| "Does not diagnose"                        | **Table stakes**                                        | Universal, and legally load-bearing for staying out of medical-device scope.                                                                    |
| De-id gate before the LLM                  | **Differentiating, but only if precisely worded**       | Four rivals say "de-identified" — mostly about the _training_ pipeline, not the _inference_ path. The distinction is the whole claim.           |
| ID-constrained citations                   | **Differentiating in form, occupied in substance**      | Abridge, Heidi and Microsoft all ship curated-corpus citations. Malaysian CPG citations are already occupied by Qmed's MOH-co-developed AskCPG. |
| Deterministic red flags (LLM may only add) | **Genuinely differentiating — and absent-for-a-reason** | No scribe vendor does it. The reason is regulatory: it is what turns a Class I documentation tool into Class B/IIa clinical decision support.   |
| Browser-side ASR                           | **Differentiating, narrow, with a quality cost**        | Everyone else uploads audio. But everyone else also deletes it, so the marginal trust gain is smaller than it looks.                            |
| All-Singapore hosting                      | **Strongest verifiable differentiator**                 | Verified absence: no vendor in the reference set offers an ASEAN in-region option. Freed is US-only, Nabla is US-or-Belgium, Corti is EU-or-US. |

**Information Assist** is Microsoft's answer to the citation problem, and its refusals map almost exactly onto CatatMD's riskiest feature ([transparency note — Information Assist](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/industry/healthcare/dragon-copilot/whitepapers/transparency-information-assist)):

- Sources are hard-constrained: "Research queries are limited only to **vetted sources that have been approved, tested, and included in users source list**," orchestrated by Azure Healthcare Agent Service and passed through "a **Clinical Safeguards engine** which filters for harmful/non-healthcare related content, hallucinations, and omissions and assures provenance."
- And then it refuses the interesting questions outright: "Information Assist **will not provide a diagnosis or specific medical advice**." The worked examples of blocked prompts include **"Are there any diagnoses I have missed?"**, "What diagnosis did I need to look at but forgot to ask?", "Does this patient need follow-up for anything else?" — because "the query could be **unsafe or inappropriate to respond to, such as medical advice or clinical decision support or diagnosis**. These would be outside of the scope of the intend use of the tool."

That last quote is the single most important line in this document for CatatMD. See §4.

## 3. SEA / Malaysia-Specific Players

This section searched hard and found less than the US/EU set — but what it found matters more, because two of the three occupy ground CatatMD was planning to claim.

### 3.1 DocReport Malaysia / Singapore — The Direct Competitor

DocReport operates localised pages for Malaysia and Singapore. The Malaysian page markets itself as "the #1 B2B ambient AI medical scribe built for Malaysian clinics and specialists," "100% PDPA aligned" ([docreport.eu/my](https://docreport.eu/my)).

**Its architecture claims are near-identical to CatatMD's de-identification story:**

- "**Browser-Side PII Redaction**": patient identifiers scrubbed locally before transmission.
- Patient names, **NRIC/passport numbers**, and phone numbers "replaced with **high-entropy tokens**" in browser memory before network transit ([docreport.eu/my](https://docreport.eu/my)).
- Singapore page: "the browser automatically redacts patient names, **NRIC/FIN numbers**, phone numbers, and dates of birth" before transmission; "**100% Client-Side PII Scrubbing in Browser**" with identifiers restored locally in browser memory; "**Zero-Knowledge Local Encryption**" (AES-GCM client-side); "Zero cleartext clinical data stored in cloud"; "The global cloud server only stores encrypted strings" ([docreport.eu/sg](https://docreport.eu/sg)).

**Its safety envelope is explicit and well-drawn** (Singapore page): "DocReport is an artificial intelligence-based clinical documentation companion… **does not make clinical decisions, issue diagnoses, or prescribe treatments. The licensed medical user retains sole responsibility for verifying, reviewing, editing, and signing off on all medical records.**" ([docreport.eu/sg](https://docreport.eu/sg))

**Pricing** (VERIFIED, both localised pages):

| Market    | Entry                                      | Claims/Billing Tier                               | Top Tier                  |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Malaysia  | **RM 499/month** (Premium, 1 doctor)       | **RM 1,499/month** (Claims Suite, billing audits) | RM 1,699/month (Ultimate) |
| Singapore | **S$299/month** (Premium, unlimited notes) | S$1,199/month (Claims)                            | S$1,299/month (Ultimate)  |

**Output and integrations:** SOAP notes in ~90 seconds, **MMA Schedule of Fees code suggestions** (Items 1101/1102), ICD-10, private-insurance denial appeals; browser-based, "fully compatible with **Plato, Klinik, GPConnect, and Socrates**"; Singapore adds "NEHR via GPConnect portal." Languages: **English, Malay, and code-switched phrases** ([docreport.eu/my](https://docreport.eu/my), [docreport.eu/sg](https://docreport.eu/sg)).

**Caveat worth carrying into the proposal (INFERRED).** The parent site is a German company, "be.smart" / DocReport AI, selling primarily into German-speaking practices with "Made in Germany" and "EU-based servers"; billing routes through "Be Smart Global, LLC" and a US Stripe account ([docreport.eu](https://docreport.eu/), [docreport.eu/my](https://docreport.eu/my)). Neither localised page states a Malaysian or Singaporean hosting region — the residency claim is carried entirely by the client-side redaction and client-side encryption, not by where the servers are. That is a real architectural position, but it is **not** in-region residency, and it is a gap CatatMD can name.

### 3.2 Qmed Asia — AskCPG (The Citations Incumbent)

Qmed Asia is a Malaysian healthtech founded 2018, serving 4,000+ providers ([Tatler Asia](https://www.tatlerasia.com/gen-t/leadership/health-tech-revolution-qmed-asias-journey-from-kiosk-solutions-to-ai-assisted-diagnosis)).

**AskCPG** is directly adjacent to CatatMD's citation feature ([qmed.ai/askcpg](https://qmed.ai/askcpg)):

- "Clinical guideline intelligence engine that enables doctors and nurses to query **Malaysian Clinical Practice Guidelines** using natural language and receive structured, **citation-linked** responses."
- "**Co-developed with the Ministry of Health Malaysia** through the Malaysian Health Technology Assessment Section (**MaHTAS**)," published on `mymahtas.moh.gov.my`.
- Responses "mapped to source sections"; corpus constrained to MaHTAS-curated Malaysian CPGs.
- "**Free for public healthcare workers.**"
- Safety disclaimers, data residency and hosting: **NOT FOUND** on the page.

Qmed also ships **Qmed Copilot** (medical chatbot: research, summaries, referral letters), Qmed AI Vision (imaging) and LabLens ([Tatler](https://www.tatlerasia.com/gen-t/leadership/health-tech-revolution-qmed-asias-journey-from-kiosk-solutions-to-ai-assisted-diagnosis), [Qmed Intelligence Suite](https://hello.qmed.asia/intelligence-suite)).

**Implication:** "AI that cites Malaysian clinical practice guidelines" is not an open position. It is held by a local incumbent with the MOH relationship and a free tier for public healthcare. CatatMD's defensible angle is the _structural_ one — IDs drawn from a supplied corpus, free-text references rejected at parse time — not the _subject matter_.

## 4. The Regulatory Wall — Why Red Flags and Suggestions Are "Absent for a Reason"

The single most consequential finding of this stream is that the features CatatMD treats as differentiators are the features every major vendor has deliberately _removed_, and the reason is uniform.

**The evidence chain:**

1. **Summarisation alone already triggers device status in the UK.** In June 2025 NHS England issued a national priority notification classifying most ambient voice technology as medical devices; "any AVT solutions that generate summaries required an MHRA Class 1 medical devices status" ([Medical Republic, 25 Jun 2025](https://www.medicalrepublic.com.au/nhs-drops-medical-device-bomb-on-ai-scribes-in-uk/117826)).
2. **Vendors stay Class I by refusing decision support.** Heidi is a self-declared Class I registration for _summarisation_, and the tracker records that it "states it does not generate diagnoses" ([iatrox](https://www.iatrox.com/blog/ai-scribe-regulatory-status-tracker-2026)). Microsoft hard-codes an intent classifier that rejects "clinical decision-making" prompts ([transparency](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/industry/healthcare/dragon-copilot/whitepapers/transparency)).
3. **Vendors that do decision support get certified as Class IIa.** Tortus: UKCA Class IIa via an Approved Body, scope "documentation plus clinical decision-support." Tandem Health: EU MDR Class IIa for scribe, coding assistant and CDS ([iatrox](https://www.iatrox.com/blog/ai-scribe-regulatory-status-tracker-2026), [tortus.ai](https://tortus.ai/)).
4. **Geography does not rescue the argument.** Malaysia applies ASEAN AMDD classification rules through the MDA. Non-critical clinical decision support software falls at **Class B minimum**, escalating to Class C where software "directly influences real-time care in urgent or high-risk clinical scenarios"; Class B requires a CAB conformity assessment plus MDA registration under MDA/GD/0041, with a Verification Route via Singapore's SMDR under MDA/GD/0070 ([TrustedTraceMed summary of MDA guidance](https://trustedtracemed.com/resources/malaysia-mda-medical-device-registration.html); classification updated in MDA/GD/0062 3rd ed., June 2025 per [Asia Actual](https://asiaactual.com/blog/asean-medical-device-classification-2025/) — **INFERRED**, secondary sources; the MDA portal hosts primary SaMD material at [portal.mda.gov.my](https://portal.mda.gov.my/index.php/documents/ukk/3664-training-7-training-on-fundamental-of-software-as-medical-device-samd/file)).

**Read against CatatMD:** red-flag detection _is_ real-time risk stratification, and cited clinical suggestions _are_ decision support. Microsoft explicitly blocks the prompt "Are there any diagnoses I have missed?" as "unsafe or inappropriate to respond to, such as medical advice or clinical decision support or diagnosis" ([Information Assist](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/industry/healthcare/dragon-copilot/whitepapers/transparency-information-assist)). CatatMD's missing-information prompts sit next to that line; whether they cross it depends entirely on whether they are framed as _documentation completeness_ ("no duration recorded for the cough") or _clinical completeness_ ("you did not ask about haemoptysis"). The second is decision support. The first is not.

This is not a reason to cut the features. It is a reason to say so first, in the proposal, before the reviewer does — and to reframe the architecture as the thing that makes a Class B path _tractable_: a versioned deterministic trigger list is testable and auditable in a way a prompt is not; ID-constrained citations make hallucinated references structurally impossible rather than statistically unlikely; the doctor-approval state transition is the risk control.

### 5.2 Genuinely Differentiating

1. **In-region ASEAN residency for the entire stack.** The verified negatives are strong and quotable: Freed is "stored within the United States"; Nabla puts every non-US client in **Belgium**; Corti offers "EU or US with no cross-border transfer"; Heidi localises to AU/CA/US/UK only; Abridge processes "within the US and countries where our service providers are located." One caveat before asserting universality: Medow Health's Singapore launch reportedly stores data on local Azure (INFERRED, secondary source).
2. **De-identification on the inference path, enforced at a single egress point.** Every rival's de-identification claim that could be pinned down is about the **training or feedback** pipeline: Freed ("AI is only trained on de-identified notes"), Suki (applied to "data designated for ML training and product improvement"), Nabla (opt-in feedback sharing). Heidi's "de-identifies and encrypts all data on the platform" is the one broad claim, and it is **unspecified as to what is stripped and when** — no vendor publishes a request-scoped vault, a re-hydration step, or an egress client that _refuses_ un-gated input. The differentiator is not the word; it is the architectural enforcement and the fact that it is inspectable.
3. **Deterministic red flags the model cannot suppress.** No scribe vendor does this at all. Corti's protocol-driven triage is the only structural cousin, and it is a different product in a different setting. The specific invariant — rules engine first, LLM as additive-only second pass, versioned trigger list — was found nowhere in this survey.
4. **Browser-side ASR.** No competitor claims audio never leaves the device. Tortus's "zero at rest" and Nabla's "we don't store audio" are retention claims, not transmission claims. **But** the differentiation is narrower than it feels — the rest of the market has already neutralised the audio-privacy objection through deletion — and it carries a real quality cost against cloud ASR on code-switched Malay/English, which DocReport already claims to handle.

## 7. What This Changes

Concrete deltas. Each is a specific edit, not a theme.

1. **Rewrite the de-identification claim to be precise, or lose it.** "We de-identify before the LLM" reads as parity — Heidi, Suki, Nabla and Freed all use that word. Change the PRD and README wording to the load-bearing version: _de-identification is applied on the **inference** path, before every outbound call, enforced at a single egress point that rejects un-gated input, with a request-scoped vault and re-hydration._ Then cite the contrast: every competitor claim that could be pinned down is a **training-pipeline** control. This is a one-paragraph edit with the highest ratio of credibility gained to effort spent.

2. **Add a "Regulatory Posture" section to the PRD/proposal.** State plainly: red flags plus cited suggestions constitute clinical decision support; under Malaysia's MDA (ASEAN AMDD rules) that is Class B territory requiring CAB conformity assessment and registration; the prototype is not placed on the market. Then argue the architecture _is_ the compliance strategy — a versioned deterministic trigger list is testable and auditable, ID-constrained citations make hallucinated references structurally impossible, doctor approval is the documented risk control. Evidence to cite: Microsoft's intent classifier rejecting clinical-decision-making prompts, Heidi's UK/EU in-session block, Tortus's Class IIa certification. **Without this section, the strongest reviewer question has no answer; with it, the answer arrives first.**

3. **Name DocReport and Qmed AskCPG in the competitive section.** Claiming "browser-side Malaysian PII tokenisation" or "citations to Malaysian guidelines" as novel is falsifiable in one search. Position against them instead: DocReport carries no stated in-region hosting (parent is German, EU servers, US billing entity) and its redaction is a client-side claim with no published enforcement; AskCPG is a standalone Q&A engine, not constrained citation inside a note under a doctor-approval gate.

4. **Re-tier the differentiation list in the proposal.** Move "doctor approves everything" and "does not diagnose" out of the differentiator column into a hygiene/safety-envelope column — every competitor states both. Keep them only in the enforced form: an explicit state transition with an audit event, not a UI convention. Promote **all-Singapore residency** to the lead differentiator, because it is the only claim in the set that is both strong and verifiable by a third party in under five minutes.

5. **Decide explicitly on note-to-transcript traceability.** Abridge's Linked Evidence and Dragon's "Summarize evidence … from the transcript" plus versioning are the top-of-market trust features, and CatatMD lists neither. Either add a lightweight version (each SOAP field carries the transcript span it came from — cheap, and it composes with the de-id vault) or scope it out in one sentence with a reason. Silence reads as an oversight.

6. **Flag browser-side Whisper as a demo risk, not just a privacy win.** Rivals compete on word error rate with cloud ASR; DocReport already claims English, Malay and code-switched phrases. A small in-browser model on code-switched Malaysian speech is the most likely thing to look bad in a live demo. Either constrain the demo to clean English audio and say why, or keep the text-transcript path as the primary demo route with browser ASR as the privacy story.

7. **Sharpen "missing-information prompts" to documentation completeness.** "No duration recorded for the cough" is documentation. "You did not ask about haemoptysis" is decision support — the exact prompt class Microsoft refuses. Pick the first framing in the PRD copy and the UI labels; it costs nothing and removes the most avoidable regulatory exposure in the product.

**Not changed:** the deterministic-red-flags-first architecture, the ID-constrained citation design, and the provider-swappable LLM adapter. The research validates all three as genuinely unoccupied. They just need to be presented as _safety engineering that makes a regulated path tractable_, rather than as features nobody thought of.
