# Data Protection Impact Assessment

**Status:** Draft engineering input for legal and operational review.

This assessment describes the proposed production flow, not the current data set. The prototype processes simulated consultation data only. It must not accept real patient data until the open actions in this document, including the owner-assigned retention period, are closed.

This is not legal advice, a legal sign-off, or a substitute for review by Malaysian privacy counsel and the clinic that will act as data controller.

## Purpose And Scope

The proposed service turns an adult GP consultation transcript into a doctor-reviewed clinical note, missing-information prompts, red-flag candidates, and cited suggestions. It does not diagnose, select treatment, or approve a note automatically. A medication the doctor dictates is recorded and confirmed by them, never generated, dose-checked, or substituted (`decisions.md` D-001).

This assessment covers:

- **Data Subjects:** Patients, doctors, and account users.
- **Personal Data:** Account identity and contact data, session data, consultation transcripts, clinical notes, analysis, and audit identifiers.
- **Sensitive Data:** Consultation content and health information. Treat it as sensitive personal data even where direct identifiers have been removed.
- **Lifecycle:** Browser collection, API processing, database storage, model processing, audit logging, retrieval, access requests, and deletion.

## Plain-Language De-Identification Statement

**Removing identifiers does not anonymise health information. De-identification is risk reduction, not anonymisation.**

The model receives a tokenised transcript, not a safe anonymous dataset. Clinical facts, rare combinations, context, and a recipient's other information can still make a person identifiable or linkable. The original transcript, re-hydrated note, and analysis remain sensitive personal data at rest. Every production control in this document therefore treats de-identified content as protected health information.

### Hosted ASR Relay: Built, And Reachable In Production

The hosted ASR relay (`POST /api/asr/transcriptions`, `trd.md` section 20, issue #154) is implemented. It sends one consultation recording, which is raw audio and voice data that cannot be de-identified first, through the API to ILMU for transcription. The relay holds the audio in request-scoped memory only, persists nothing, logs no content, writes paired audit events (`asr.hosted_relayed`, `asr.hosted_relay_failed`), and is disabled entirely (HTTP 503) on any deployment where `ILMU_API_KEY` is absent. Stated precisely, and corrected 16/08/26: the per-consultation consent interaction shipped with #155, and `ILMU_API_KEY` is set in the Render production service, so this path is **reachable in production today**. Any authenticated user who selects the hosted engine and ticks the consent box sends that recording to ILMU, and that includes the shared guest demo account, which is open to self-service sign-in.

**Corrected 06/09/26 (#254).** Between 02/09/26 and 06/09/26 the per-consultation interaction did not exist. #228 replaced it with a standing device preference held in `localStorage`, so for three weeks the only gate on this path was a setting that, once chosen, applied to every subsequent patient on that device without asking any of them. Two consequences worth stating plainly, because neither is fixed by the restoration:

- **The gap was real while it lasted.** Any hosted recording taken on this deployment in that window was sent under a remembered preference, not a per-patient agreement. All consultation data in this project is simulated, so no real patient was affected.
- **Consent remains client-side only.** The API enforces no consent signal and cannot: it receives audio and a session, and has no way to distinguish an agreed recording from an unagreed one. The gate is a property of the frontend, and the audit trail records the choice rather than proving it. That is the same limitation the relay shipped with and is unchanged by #254. An earlier revision of this section said the path was held closed by leaving the key unset; that is no longer true and was already out of date when written. This section and the ILMU processor row below are the DPIA update that introducing the path requires; the release gate for real data below stands unchanged, and processor verification for ILMU remains open.

## Consent And Permitted Processing

The proposed operational baseline is explicit patient consent before a doctor submits any real consultation data. Consent must be voluntary, specific to the stated service, recorded, and withdrawable through the clinic's request channel. A patient must not be asked to consent to LLM training, marketing, or unrelated reuse as a condition of care.

Before a clinic relies on another permitted-processing basis or a sensitive-data exception, Malaysian counsel must identify and document it. Engineering must not infer a legal basis from the fact that the product is clinically useful or that a doctor has an account.

The consent and notice record must identify:

- **Controller And Contact:** The clinic, its privacy contact, and the service operator's role.
- **Purpose And Data:** Consultation documentation, reviewable decision support, the categories of health and account data, and no autonomous clinical decision.
- **Recipients And Transfers:** Render, Supabase, Alibaba Model Studio, ILMU where the hosted-ASR choice is used, and any confirmed Vercel, Cloudflare, or other sub-processor path.
- **Choices And Consequences:** Local-only or non-service alternatives where available, the hosted-ASR per-consultation choice (#155, restored #254), and the consequences of withdrawal.
- **Rights Channel:** How the patient requests access, correction, withdrawal, deletion where permissible, or raises a complaint.

## Retention, Deletion, And Access Requests

### Open Retention Decision

The retention period remains an owner-assigned decision. The prototype now carries a **configurable default reflecting the common clinical-records retention convention, which the clinic data controller must review and adopt or override.**

**What The Prototype Holds**

| Element                | State                                                                                                   |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Default offered        | Seven years, as a starting value shown for review in Settings.                                          |
| Status of that default | **Not a legal determination**, not a statutory requirement, and not a citation of any law.              |
| Storage                | `User.retentionYears`, per doctor, whole years. Null until the controller adopts a period.              |
| Unadopted state        | Null is a real state and never reads as seven. The UI shows "not yet adopted" and "adopted" separately. |
| Enforcement            | **None.** No retention job, no TTL, no backup expiry, no deletion sweep reads this value.               |

Recording the decision and acting on it are separate pieces of work. Only the first is built.

**Recommended default:** Do not enable real-data processing until the clinic data controller has reviewed and adopted a written retention period. That period should be no longer than the minimum clinical-record retention requirement applicable to the clinic's records, verified by Malaysian legal and professional-recordkeeping advice. The stored default is a starting point for that review, not a substitute for it.

The decision must separately cover consultation data, patient records, account and session data, audit events, database backups, platform logs, and consent or request records. It must name the legal or operational rationale, the deletion trigger, and the accountable owner.

Adoption and withdrawal are both audited (`settings.retention_adopted`, carrying the value), so the period in force on a given date, and the actor who set it, are answerable from the trail rather than from the current column value alone.

### Consultation Audio

The recording behind a transcript is held so a doctor can check what the recogniser wrote (#293). It is the only PHI in the system that **cannot be de-identified**, because a voice is an identifier in its own right, and the only PHI that expires on a clock.

| Property           | State                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Legal character    | Treated as a working artefact, not the medical record. **The approved note is the record.** See the open question below. |
| Adoption           | **720 hours (30 days), adopted 09/09/26** on the Render API service by the deployment owner. See below.                  |
| Storage            | `consultation_audio`, a table of its own so no ordinary consultation read loads it. Never sent to any provider.          |
| Served past expiry | Never. The read path checks the clock itself rather than trusting that a sweep ran.                                      |
| Deletion, expiry   | `sweepExpiredAudio`, run opportunistically on upload, which is the only way the store grows. Audited as `audio.swept`.   |
| Deletion, erasure  | A real delete, not a tombstone, ahead of `consultation.erased`. Audited as `consultation.audio_purged`.                  |
| Deletion, approval | **None.** Approving a note does not destroy the recording; the window is what bounds it.                                 |
| Access             | The owning doctor only, through `assertOwnedConsultation`. There is no role model, so no one else can reach it.          |

**The adopted period, and what it is and is not.**

| Decision       | Detail                                                                                                                                                                                |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Value in force | `AUDIO_RETENTION_HOURS=720`, that is 30 days                                                                                                                                          |
| Adopted        | 09/09/26, set on the Render API service. Not committed to the repository: `render.yaml` declares the key `sync: false` with no value, so the number lives where the decision was made |
| Basis          | The published window of Abridge and Nuance DAX, chosen because it is the market norm rather than a figure invented here. Nabla's is 14 days                                           |
| Status         | **A working convention, not a legal determination.** No statute was consulted and none is cited for it                                                                                |
| Review trigger | Any answer to the cl.19 question below, and any move from simulated to real consultation data                                                                                         |

Unset remains a real state and the code still fails closed on it: nothing is stored, the write answers 503 and the read 404. Adopting a number turned the feature on; withdrawing it turns storage off and stops recordings being served, though it does not itself delete what is already stored (see the sweep's limits under Required Deletion Mechanism).

**Why approval does not delete it.** Abridge and Nuance DAX both keep audio for a fixed window regardless of when the note is signed, and a doctor who has signed is exactly the person a colleague or patient returns to with a question. The alternative, deleting at approval, is tighter and is what the 2026 Frontiers policy paper recommends; it was considered and not taken. Recorded here because it is a controller-visible choice, not an implementation detail.

**Open question for the controller.** MMC Guideline 003/2023 cl.19 states that recordings made for clinical purposes constitute part of a patient's medical record, which would pull a stored recording into medical-record retention rather than this window. The vendor reading, that a draft-verification recording is a working artefact outside the record, has not been tested in Malaysia. Cl.18 separately requires consent specific to the purpose, with an assurance of secure storage, and cl.13 entitles the patient to a copy. **None of those three are built.** Resolve before any real-data processing. Sources in `docs/superpowers/research/10-audio-evidence-playback.md`.

### Required Deletion Mechanism

**What Exists Today**

| Mechanism                         | State                                                                                                    |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `POST /api/consultations/erase`   | Built. Batch tombstone erasure of the caller's own consultations.                                        |
| `POST /api/patients/:id/erase`    | Built (PR #212). Tombstone erasure of a patient record, cascading to that patient's consultations.       |
| Retention period                  | Recorded per doctor, unadopted by default, and enforced by nothing. See "Open Retention Decision" above. |
| Consultation audio window         | Built (#293). The one thing in the system that expires on a clock. See "Consultation Audio" below.       |
| Retention job, TTL, backup expiry | **Not built** for clinical text. Nothing but the audio window above expires on a schedule.               |
| Identity-verified access requests | **Not built.** There is no request channel and no verification workflow.                                 |

Both erasure endpoints null the PHI columns and stamp `erasedAt`. Neither deletes a row, because `AuditEvent` chains on record ids and a hard delete would break tamper evidence.

Before production, implement and test a controller-authorised workflow that:

1. Verifies the requester's identity without placing new health information in ordinary support channels.
2. Locates the subject's consultation records, account records, sessions, audit records, and linked provider or platform records.
3. Applies the controller's retention decision, including any lawful requirement to retain a medical record or minimal audit evidence.
4. Deletes or irreversibly de-identifies eligible data in the primary database. The `Consultation` to `AuditEvent` relation is `onDelete: Restrict`, so a consultation holding audit rows cannot be hard-deleted at all (issue #64). Erasure is a tombstone that nulls the PHI columns and keeps the row, which is what preserves the tamper-evident hash chain. The policy must decide what, if anything, ever removes the audit evidence itself.
5. Expires copies from backups and platform logs on a documented schedule, with restoration controls that prevent deleted data from reappearing unnoticed.
6. Records request ID, decision, authority, scope, completion date, and exceptions without recording transcript or note content.
7. Obtains deletion confirmation and timing from each processor where its systems retain relevant data.

### Required Access And Correction Mechanism

Before production, the clinic must offer a documented request channel and service-level workflow for access, correction, withdrawal, and deletion requests. The workflow must:

- **Authenticate Safely:** Verify identity and authority, including a representative's authority where applicable.
- **Route To The Controller:** Require a clinic decision for medical-record correction, retention exceptions, and disclosure.
- **Provide A Bounded Export:** Return the permitted consultation and account data through a secure, authenticated channel.
- **Preserve Provenance:** Record corrections as an attributable correction or amendment, rather than silently overwriting a clinical record.
- **Track Deadlines And Outcomes:** Maintain a request register without clinical-content copies and escalate unresolved requests to the controller's privacy contact.

## Release Gate And Review Cycle

Real patient data remains out of scope until all of the following are evidenced:

- **Retention Approval:** The clinic data controller assigns the documented period and deletion exceptions.
- **Rights Workflow:** Access, correction, withdrawal, and deletion processes are implemented and tested.
- **Vendor Assurance:** DPAs, sub-processor lists, processing regions, deletion commitments, and support-access controls are reviewed.
- **Cross-Border Record:** The transfer impact assessment and section 129 basis are recorded for every non-Malaysian recipient.
- **Consent And Notice:** Patient-facing notice and consent record are approved for the operating clinic.
- **DPIA Review:** Malaysian privacy counsel and the clinic complete their review before production deployment and whenever a new processor, country, model provider, or hosted-ASR path is introduced.

### Does Ambient Capture Trigger This Gate? Yes

Recorded 06/09/26, because the question was asked and an unwritten answer is the same failure this document exists to prevent.

**On the wording**, "hosted-ASR path" is enumerated separately from "processor", so keeping the same processor does not satisfy the clause. Ambient capture is by definition a new hosted-ASR path.

**On the substance**, which matters more, this was originally assessed against a design that kept the same processor. **That is no longer the case (revised 06/09/26, `trd.md` §20.10): ambient capture goes to Soniox, in the United States.** The one bullet the same-processor fact used to answer is now open too, and it is the heaviest of the three.

| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Vendor Assurance    | **Yes.** A new processor, a new country and the same data type. A full sub-processor inventory, DPA and transfer terms are required, and the vendor's no-storage and no-training statements are read from their documentation and unverified by us. Nothing is left of what the same-processor fact used to buy                                                                                   |
| Consent And Notice  | **Yes.** The approved consent record covers a per-recording gesture.
